Common Questions

What importers ask first.

Plain answers on eligibility, cost, timing, and risk.

Who qualifies for a refund?

Broadly, any U.S. business that paid tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) on goods entered in 2025. The 2026 Supreme Court ruling held those specific tariffs were imposed without proper authorization. Whether your individual entries are recoverable — and through which pathway — depends on your entry dates, liquidation status, and filing history. That is exactly what the free eligibility review determines.

What does it cost?

The eligibility review is free. If counsel confirms in writing that your entries are recoverable, a $10,000 engagement fee begins the formal representation. A 20% contingency fee on the amount actually recovered is due within 15 days of your business receiving the refund.

If no refund is recovered, no contingency fee is owed. Exact terms are set out in the written engagement agreement before any work begins.

Why do I need attorneys instead of my customs broker?

Customs brokers handle operational filing — entry classification, CAPE submissions, and day-to-day work with CBP. Recovering unlawfully collected tariffs is a legal matter: it can require administrative protests, litigation before the U.S. Court of International Trade, preserving claims past statutory deadlines, and protection under attorney-client privilege. Brokers are not authorized to practice law, and improperly filed claims carry audit-recapture risk.

How long until I receive a refund?

It depends on the pathway. Entries handled through the CAPE portal (Phase 1) are currently processing in roughly 60–90 days. Historically liquidated entries (Phase 2) can take 12–18 months. Matters that require litigation can take longer. Counsel will give you a realistic timeline for your specific entries during the review.

Is there a deadline?

Yes — and it is the reason timing matters. Administrative protests generally must be filed within 180 days of an entry's liquidation date, and a government appeal deadline of June 7 affects how some claims should be structured. Missing these windows can permanently forfeit otherwise recoverable amounts. The sooner your entries are reviewed, the more pathways remain open.

Is the refund guaranteed?

No. Refunds are not guaranteed. Outcomes depend on your specific entries, filing history, and how the government's pending appeals resolve. Roughly 40% of early CAPE filings have been rejected, often for avoidable procedural reasons — which is precisely why the work is handled by specialist counsel.

What information do I need to provide?

To start, just your business details and a rough sense of your 2025 import activity. For the full review, counsel will request entry summaries (CBP Form 7501), commercial invoices, and records of duties paid. The intake form takes most businesses 15–30 minutes; counsel will tell you exactly what else is needed.
